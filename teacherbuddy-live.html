What is TeacherBuddy?

A short setup, then it runs quietly in the background of your school day.

TeacherBuddy listens through the school day, then turns it into a short, honest reflection — achievements, anecdotes, and the moments that pulled you off track — so you can see your own day clearly and talk it through, one week at a time.

Everyone's audio is handled with care, but it's not entirely on-device: live speech-to-text during Live the Day sends your classroom's audio content to your browser's speech-recognition service to generate text. Step 4 explains exactly what that means before you start.

Step 4 of 4 — Privacy & consent

Privacy, consent, and FERPA

A quick check before you start recording a real classroom.

What TeacherBuddy records and keeps

Live the Day works differently. While you're recording, TeacherBuddy uses your browser's built-in live speech-to-text (Chrome's "Web Speech" feature) to generate a transcript as you teach, and to auto-flag possible achievements and notable moments from it. To do that, your classroom's audio content is sent to Google's speech-recognition servers to be converted into text — it does leave this device for that one purpose, even though TeacherBuddy itself has no server of its own. The raw audio recording stays on this device only, for you to review or download when you stop recording — it is never uploaded.
Achievements and anecdotal records are flagged automatically using simple keyword matching on the transcript, not real understanding of your lesson — review and remove anything inaccurate before you share a weekly report.
TeacherBuddy also flags a smaller set of "needs a follow-up" moments — tool or tech trouble, and possible conflicts between students — and will ask you, in Reflection, to explain what actually happened. Your typed explanation is attached to that moment. A flagged student conflict is, in effect, a note about a student's behavior — treat it with the same care as anything else you'd write about a student, and feel free to remove it if it's inaccurate or you'd rather not keep it.
What goes to Claude, Anthropic's AI, and when. Nothing is sent until you tap a button. On Day's End, Read my day sends that day's transcript and the times of its quiet stretches, so Claude can suggest what each stretch of talk was and which short sounds inside quiet time were only brief interruptions. Summarize my day sends that day's transcript, your quiet-time tags, and your explanations of flagged moments, so Claude can write a short summary and suggest reflection questions. When you answer those questions, your answers are sent so TeacherBuddy can ask at most one clarifying question and then sum up your reflection. On Friday's Week Reflection, your answer about work outside school hours is sent so it can be sorted into time categories. Anthropic's API doesn't use this data to train its models and deletes it after 30 days. Claude only reflects back what you said. It doesn't give feedback or judge your teaching.
Speaking your answers. If you tap Speak, your browser's speech recognition turns your voice into text, the same way live transcription works. You see the text and can fix it before sending. No audio of your answers is recorded or kept.
Typed reflections are stored on this device, until you choose to download a PDF report and send it to your coach yourself.
This is a personal prototype, not a school system. It isn't connected to your school's student information system, and no one else can see anything here unless you choose to share it.

Your responsibility before recording

Because this records real audio of your classroom and sends that audio content to a third-party speech-recognition service to generate text, student voices will likely be captured and processed off-device, not just recorded locally. FERPA and your school or district's own policies govern how student education records are collected, used, and shared — an app setting can't determine that for you. Check with your school on their requirements before using live transcription with real students, and only record when you have the appropriate notice or consent in place for your classroom.
When writing reflections you plan to share with your coach, consider referring to students by role or initials rather than full names.
